---
title: "The Last Row — Scene 10: After‑Dark"
date: "2026-05-15"
description: "The performance is over. The audience is gone. Four people sit on the edge of the stage, sharing water and silence. The critic is no longer a critic."
---

**SETTING:**
The stage, post‑performance. The house lights are up, but dimmed to a sleepy half‑glow. The audience has gone. The six chairs have been pushed to one side of the stage, stacked carelessly. The scrim is unlit, just a pale grey sheet now. A single work light on a stand illuminates the centre of the stage. The ghost light has been plugged in and glows faintly downstage. The theatre is silent except for the distant hum of the ventilation system and, from somewhere far off, the sound of the last audience members leaving the building.

**TIME:**
Thirty minutes after curtain call.

**AT RISE:**
MARTIN, KEIRA, JAMAL, and LEO sit on the edge of the stage, legs dangling over the lip, facing the empty auditorium. They are arranged in a loose row: MARTIN on one end, then LEO, then JAMAL, then KEIRA. Someone has produced a bottle of water, which they pass between them. No one speaks. The silence isn't uncomfortable — it's the silence of people who have been through something together and don't yet have the words for it.

Martin's jacket is draped over his lap. His reading glasses are pushed up into his hair. He looks exhausted. He looks, for the first time all night, at peace.

---

**JAMAL** *(breaking the silence, staring out at the empty seats)*
I got the thunderclap right.

*A beat. Then LEO laughs — a tired, giddy, disbelieving laugh. KEIRA shakes her head, smiling.*

**KEIRA**
You got everything right.

**JAMAL**
No, but — *(he turns to her, earnest)* — the thunderclap. The one I messed up in rehearsal. I got it right tonight. Right on cue. Right volume. No one jumped.

**MARTIN** *(without turning his head)*
I jumped.

**LEO**
Me too.

**JAMAL** *(deflating slightly)*
Oh.

**KEIRA**
Jumping is different from being startled by a mistake. Jumping means you're in it. *(she nudges Jamal with her elbow)* It was a good thunderclap.

**JAMAL** *(a small, pleased smile)*
Yeah?

**MARTIN**
It was an excellent thunderclap, Jamal.

*JAMAL's smile widens. He looks down at his hands, still marked from the lighting board, and seems to decide something about himself.*

---

*Another silence, this one easier. LEO takes the water bottle, drinks, passes it to MARTIN. MARTIN takes it, drinks, stares at the empty stage.*

**LEO** *(carefully)*
You were good tonight.

**MARTIN**
I forgot my line.

**LEO**
I know. That was the best part.

*MARTIN turns to look at him. LEO doesn't flinch.*

**LEO**
I mean it. The whole play was building to something — we were building to something — and then you just... stopped. You stopped being the Builder. You stopped being a critic. You were just a man on a stage who didn't know what came next. *(he shrugs)* That's theatre. That's the thing I've been chasing for four years and forty‑seven auditions. And you did it by accident.

**MARTIN** *(a small, rueful laugh)*
I've spent thirty years teaching myself precision. Control. The right word in the right place. And the best thing I did tonight was forget.

**KEIRA**
That's not the only thing you did.

*She says it simply, not as reassurance, but as fact. MARTIN looks at her.*

**KEIRA**
You apologised. Not just to the audience — to Jamal, to Leo, to the actors who weren't even here. You stood on a stage and said you were wrong. I've been working in theatre for fifteen years. I've never seen a critic do that. I've never seen anyone do that.

**MARTIN** *(quietly)*
It wasn't enough.

**KEIRA**
It was a start.

*She holds his gaze. He doesn't look away. The ghost light flickers, just once, then steadies.*

---

*JAMAL, who has been fidgeting with something in his pocket, suddenly produces a crumpled piece of paper.*

**JAMAL**
I found this. In the back row. After everyone left.

*He holds it out. It's the folded rejection letter — the one MARTIN placed on the chair during Scene 9. MARTIN stares at it.*

**MARTIN**
Someone left it.

**JAMAL**
Someone left it face‑up. On the seat. Like they'd read it. And then they put it back.

*MARTIN takes the letter from JAMAL. He unfolds it. There's something written on it that wasn't there before — a single line in unfamiliar handwriting, in pen, below the original rejection text.*

**MARTIN** *(reading aloud)*
"Thank you for not hiding."

*His voice catches. He stares at the words. The others lean in, reading over his shoulder.*

**LEO**
Who wrote that?

**KEIRA** *(after a pause)*
The woman in the back row. The one who laughed.

*MARTIN folds the letter again, very carefully, and tucks it into his jacket pocket. He doesn't speak for a moment.*

**MARTIN** *(at last)*
I thought the back row was where you went to be invisible. To judge without being judged. I thought it was the safest place in the theatre. *(he looks out at the empty seats, at the back row, at the ghost light's small, steady glow)* It turns out it's just another seat. With the same view as everywhere else. You just have to turn your head.

*He turns to the others — Leo, Jamal, Keira — his company, his collaborators, the people who stayed.*

**MARTIN**
I don't know what happens next. I said that on stage and I meant it. I don't know if this play has a future, or if I do, or if any of us do. But I know I want to find out. With you. If you'll have me.

*LEO grins — the same disarming, earnest grin from Scene 2, but steadier now.*

**LEO**
We already have you. You showed up. You stayed.

**JAMAL** *(nodding)*
And you didn't shout at me once during the performance.

**MARTIN**
I was busy.

**JAMAL**
I know. That's the point.

*KEIRA stands. She stretches, her clipboard dangling from one hand. She looks down at the three of them — the critic, the understudy, the tech — and something in her expression softens.*

**KEIRA**
Right. We have notes for tomorrow. The storm cue needs tightening. Leo, your Act II monologue is still about seventy percent there. Martin — *(she pauses, almost smiles)* — Martin, you need to work on your diaphragmatic breathing.

*MARTIN stares at her. Then, slowly, wonderfully, he laughs. Not the hollow, defensive laugh from Scene 1. A real one. The others join in — LEO first, then JAMAL, then KEIRA herself. The sound fills the empty theatre, bounces off the back wall, and settles somewhere in the back row.*

**MARTIN** *(wiping his eyes)*
I walked into that.

**KEIRA**
You did.

*She holds out her hand. MARTIN takes it and pulls himself to his feet. LEO and JAMAL follow. The four of them stand on the edge of the stage, looking out at the empty auditorium — the seats, the aisles, the exit signs, the ghost light. The theatre is still. The night is over. The work is just beginning.*

**MARTIN**
Keira.

**KEIRA**
What?

**MARTIN**
Thank you. For telling me the truth. In the rehearsal. When I needed it.

**KEIRA** *(a beat)*
That's my job.

**MARTIN**
No. Your job is to manage the stage. Telling me the truth was something else.

*KEIRA considers this. Then she nods — a small, private gesture that contains more than words.*

**KEIRA**
You're welcome.

---

*LEO picks up the water bottle. JAMAL picks up his laptop. KEIRA tucks her clipboard under her arm. MARTIN straightens his jacket. They don't leave immediately — they stand together for a moment, shoulder to shoulder, facing the empty theatre. Then, one by one, they turn and walk into the wings. JAMAL first, then LEO, then KEIRA. MARTIN lingers. He looks back at the ghost light, glowing alone on the stage. He walks to it. He places his hand on it, gently, as if it were a living thing. Then he unplugs it. The stage goes dark.*
