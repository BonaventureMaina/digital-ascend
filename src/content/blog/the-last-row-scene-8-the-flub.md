---
title: "The Last Row — Scene 8: The Flub"
date: "2026-05-15"
description: "Martin forgets his line. The fourth wall breaks. A critic becomes a man on a stage who doesn't know what happens next."
---

**SETTING:**
The stage, deep into the performance. The play within the play has been running for some time. The audience is with them — fully invested, forgiving of the rough edges. The set is the same: six chairs facing upstage, a scrim glowing pale blue. THE BUILDER (MARTIN) stands centre stage. THE STRANGER (LEO) is seated in one of the upstage chairs, watching. They are in the middle of a scene — the climatic confrontation between the Builder and the Stranger. The Builder is supposed to confess why he built the basement theatre. But something is wrong.

**TIME:**
Approximately the 70‑minute mark of the 90‑minute performance.

**AT RISE:**
MARTIN stands in the amber pool of light. His scripted line should come now. He knows it. He's said it a hundred times in rehearsal. But his mouth doesn't open. LEO, in character, waits. The pause extends beyond what's comfortable, beyond what's theatrical, into something else entirely.

---

**THE STRANGER** *(Leo, prompting gently)*
You said you built this place because the real theatre wouldn't have you.

*MARTIN nods. He still doesn't speak. LEO's eyes flicker — stay in character, stay in character —*

**THE STRANGER** *(Leo, a little louder, still in character)*
You said they rejected your scripts. For years. You said you started collecting the regrets in a drawer.

*MARTIN looks at LEO. He's not the Builder anymore. He's Martin. The shift is visible — a dropping of the shoulders, a loss of the character's tension. The audience senses it. A rustle moves through the house.*

**MARTIN** *(quietly, to Leo)*
I've forgotten the line.

*LEO blinks. He doesn't know if this is still the play or not.*

**LEO** *(whispering, out of character)*
"The real theatre wouldn't have me, so I built a false one." That's the next line. Say that.

*MARTIN stares at him. Then, very slowly, he turns away from LEO and faces the audience. The amber light holds him. The scrim glows. The chairs face upstage. The theatre is utterly silent.*

**MARTIN** *(to the audience)*
I have reviewed 832 productions.

*A beat. LEO freezes. This is not in the script. This is not in any script.*

**MARTIN**
I have used words like "transcendent" and "uneven" and — *(he almost smiles)* — "a bold choice." I have sat in the back row for thirty years and I have written things in a notebook that actors still remember, years later, because words have weight, and I knew exactly how much weight I was throwing.

*He takes off the wire‑rimmed glasses. He cleans them on his sleeve. It's the same gesture from the curtain speech, but now it's not a prop — it's a habit, a tell, something deeply human.*

**MARTIN**
I have never once said, in print or in person, "I don't know what happens next."

*Silence. He looks out at the audience — not the mannequins, not the characters, but the real people in the real seats.*

**MARTIN**
I don't know what happens next. This is my play. I wrote every word. And I don't know what happens next.

*A longer pause. The silence is vast. LEO doesn't move. JAMAL, in the booth, has his hands suspended above the keys, afraid to breathe. KEIRA, in the wings, has lowered her clipboard.*

**MARTIN** *(a small, wondering laugh)*
That's not a rhetorical flourish. I've forgotten the line. I had it. And now I don't.

*Someone in the audience laughs. Not loud. Not cruel. A single, soft exhale from the back row — Martin's row. The laugh of someone who recognises a human moment when they see one. MARTIN hears it. He turns toward the sound. He's wearing his reading glasses. He can see, for the first time in thirty years, the face of the person in the back row. It's an older woman. She's smiling.*

**MARTIN** *(to her, directly)*
Is this what you paid for?

*She nods, still smiling. Someone else chuckles. The tension in the room shifts — not breaking, but transforming, like ice turning to water while still holding its shape.*

**MARTIN** *(turning back to the full audience)*
I used to think the back row was the safest place in the theatre. You're far from the stage. You can't be seen. You have distance. You have — *(he searches)* — immunity. But I've just realised something. Sitting here, tonight, for the first time, on this side of the lights — *(he gestures at the stage, at himself)* — the back row isn't safe at all. It's the most exposed seat in the house. Because you're alone. You're alone with your notebook and your opinions and your carefully maintained distance, and no one can reach you. Not really. And after a while, you stop wanting them to.

*He takes a step closer to the edge of the stage. The lights stay with him — JAMAL, in the booth, is following him, hands trembling but steady enough.*

**MARTIN**
I wrote a play. It's about a man who builds a theatre in his basement and fills it with mannequins. I didn't realise, until about an hour ago, that I wrote it about myself. *(a breath)* I didn't realise, until about two minutes ago, that I also wrote it for you. For the people in the seats. The real ones. The ones who showed up.

*He looks at LEO, who is still frozen in his chair, still holding character by the thinnest thread. MARTIN gives him a small nod — we're still in this, we're still together.*

**MARTIN** *(to the audience)*
I'm supposed to be saying something now. Something about the false theatre, and the real one, and the difference between them. I wrote it. I can't remember it. *(he pauses)* But I think the difference is: you're here. That's it. That's the whole difference. You're here, and you're real, and I'm terrified of you, and I'm grateful for you, and I don't know what happens next.

*He turns back to LEO. Something passes between them — a current, a recognition. LEO rises from his chair. He knows what to do. He crosses to MARTIN and stands beside him, not as the Stranger, but as Leo. He feeds Martin the line — not whispered, not hidden, but spoken openly, for everyone to hear.*

**LEO**
"You built this place because the real theatre wouldn't have you."

*MARTIN looks at him. He takes a breath.*

**MARTIN** *(the Builder, but also Martin)*
The real theatre wouldn't have me. So I built a false one. And then I invited you in. And now — *(he looks at Leo, at the audience, at the lights, at the whole impossible, beautiful, terrifying thing)* — now I have to face you. Not the mannequins. You.

*He reaches out and, unscripted, takes LEO's hand. LEO squeezes it. The Stranger speaks his next line — the real line, the scripted one — but it comes out new.*

**THE STRANGER** *(Leo)*
We can do that. Together.

*MARTIN almost breaks. He doesn't. He holds it. He holds the moment. The amber light warms around them. The scrim glows. The chairs face upstage. The play continues.*
