title: "The Last Row — Scene 9: The Last Row"
date: "2026-05-15"
description: "Martin dismantles his fake theatre. The last chair faces the real audience. A goodbye, a thank you, and a folded piece of paper."
---

**SETTING:**
The stage. The play within the play has reached its final scene. The six chairs still face upstage, but something has changed — one of them has been turned around. It now faces the audience. The scrim glows a deep, fading amber, like the last light of a long day. THE BUILDER (MARTIN) stands alone on stage. THE STRANGER (LEO) has exited. The mannequins — invisible, imagined — are gone. The basement theatre is empty. Only the Builder remains, and the real audience beyond him.

**TIME:**
The final minutes of the play within the play.

**AT RISE:**
MARTIN, as THE BUILDER, stands in the centre of the stage. He looks at the chairs — his creation, his audience of dummies — and then he begins, very quietly, to dismantle them. He lifts one chair and moves it to the wings. Then another. He works in silence, and each chair removed is a small act of letting go.

---

**THE BUILDER** *(Martin)*
I built this place for you. *(he moves another chair)* For all of you. *(another chair)* I built it because I was afraid of the real ones. The real theatres, with their real seats and their real critics and their real, terrifying, irreducible audiences. *(he stops, holding the last chair — the one that faces the audience)* I thought if I built my own, I could control it. I could decide who watched. I could decide what they thought. I could be safe.

*He sets the last chair down. He doesn't move it to the wings. He turns it fully toward the audience, so it faces them directly. He stands behind it, his hands on its back.*

**THE BUILDER**
But the thing about a theatre — a real theatre — is that you can't control it. You can write the words. You can set the lights. You can rehearse until your voice gives out. And then the audience arrives, and everything changes. They bring themselves. They bring their bad days and their good hearts and their impossible expectations. They laugh where you didn't plan for laughter. They're silent where you hoped for applause. They make the play something you didn't write and couldn't have imagined. They make it alive.

*He walks around the chair and sits in it. He faces the audience — the real one. The lights shift, isolating him in a single warm pool.*

**THE BUILDER**
I spent years thinking the audience was the enemy. The critic. The judge. The person in the back row who writes things in a notebook that you'll remember for the rest of your life. But tonight I understand something. *(a breath)* The person in the back row is just another person. They're afraid. They're lonely. They're hoping to be moved. They're hoping, against all evidence, that the lights will go down and something true will happen. They're not the enemy. They never were. I was.

*He stands. He leaves the chair where it is — facing the audience, empty, an invitation.*

**THE BUILDER**
I'm going to close this theatre now. The basement one. The false one. I'm going to take down the lights and put away the chairs and let the mannequins go back to being what they always were — old clothes, old hopes, old fears. And then I'm going to walk upstairs and open the door and see what's out there. *(he looks at the audience, and for a moment, he is not the Builder. He is Martin. Completely, terrifyingly Martin)* Maybe nothing. Maybe everything. Either way — I'll have stopped hiding.

*He reaches into his pocket and produces a small, folded piece of paper — the rejection letter. He unfolds it. He reads it, silently, one last time. Then he folds it again and places it on the empty chair, like an offering, like a goodbye.*

**THE BUILDER** *(Martin)*
This is for you. Whoever you are. Whatever you thought of tonight. This is the last piece of the old theatre. The last regret. I'm leaving it here. You can take it if you want. You can leave it. It doesn't matter. What matters is — *(he looks up, at the back row, at the woman who laughed, at the whole silent, waiting room)* — what matters is that you came. You showed up. You sat in the dark with strangers and waited for something true. That's not nothing. That's the opposite of nothing.

*He turns and begins to walk upstage, toward the scrim, toward the exit. He pauses at the edge of the light. He looks back, one last time, at the chair — the empty one, facing the audience, holding the letter.*

**THE BUILDER** *(softer)*
Thank you.

*He walks through the scrim. It glows as he passes, then fades to darkness. The chair remains, lit, empty, waiting. The stage holds for a long, breath‑held moment. Then — blackout.*
